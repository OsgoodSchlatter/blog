I got to the base of the mountain before sunrise, while the vendors were still
stacking crates. By the time the light hit the vermilion gates, the path had
already filled with people — but for about twenty minutes, it was just me and
the mountain.

## The gates

The **senbon torii** ("thousand torii gates") aren't literally a thousand —
more like ten thousand, donated over centuries by individuals and businesses
hoping for good fortune. Each one is inscribed with the donor's name and the
date.

| Section       | Approx. gates | Elevation gain |
|---------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Base to Okusha | ~450          | 90 m            |
| Okusha to Yotsutsuji | ~600    | 150 m           |
| Yotsutsuji to summit | ~1,200  | 130 m           |

## A little math

If gates are spaced roughly evenly along the path with average spacing
$d \approx 0.9\text{ m}$, and the path to the summit is about
$L \approx 4\text{ km}$, then a rough estimate of the gate count is:

$$
N \approx \frac{L}{d} = \frac{4000}{0.9} \approx 4{,}444
$$

Which lines up reasonably well with the commonly cited figure of "thousands"
of gates, even if the popular name rounds down.

You can also express the cumulative climb as a simple sum over sections
$i = 1, \dots, n$ with elevation gain $h_i$:

$$
H_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} h_i
$$

which, per the table above, comes to about $370\text{ m}$ from base to summit.

## Practical notes

- Go before 7am if you want the gates to yourself.
- Bring cash — the small shrines along the way sell little else.
- The full loop takes 2–3 hours at an easy pace.
